# Approved 25-monument expansion

The user approved seven additional Hampi monuments, six at Pattadakal, five at Badami, five at Aihole, and one each at Halebidu and Somanathapura. Together with the five pilot records, the intended collection contains 30 monuments across seven locations.

The authoritative new data is `expansion.mjs`, combined with `pilot.json` by `dataset.mjs`. The importer preserves existing building records, including administrator edits; new records receive their own aliases, taxonomy links, citations and photographs.

## Research decisions

- Hampi's Virupaksha Temple has its own slug, separate from the same-named Pattadakal monument. Its date label describes Vijayanagara additions rather than the origin of the earlier shrine.
- Jambulinga has conflicting seventh/eighth-century attributions in two ASI accounts. Both references are retained, and the public date spans the two centuries.
- Papanatha's chronology and style terminology vary. Its date is broad; its northern-form classification is attributed to ASI's ensemble description rather than presented as an uncontested label.
- Malegitti Shivalaya follows ASI's eighth-century attribution. Photograph descriptions proposing other dates are not used as historical evidence.
- Bhutanatha refers to the principal lakeside temple. It does not duplicate the entire group as a second record.
- Huchappayya Matha is distinct from Huchappayya Gudi. The misleadingly named, low-resolution Commons file `Huchappayya (gudi) temple, Aihole, Karnataka.jpg` was excluded. The selected Matha exterior and ceiling photograph use the monument-specific collection.
- Lad Khan's disputed construction date is left unassigned. The alternate name Chalukya Shiva Temple is searchable.
- A geological material is added only where the reference supports it. Some records use the broader material Stone; remaining unknown identifications are displayed as undocumented.

## Reference additions

The expansion adds monument descriptions from ASI's Hampi and Dharwad circles, Karnataka and district tourism resources, the UNESCO Hoysala listing, and the Aihole–Badami–Pattadakal tentative-list submission. The latter is identified as a tentative-list source rather than an inscription of Aihole or Badami as World Heritage properties.

Material evidence includes ASI's December 2022 newsletter for Papanatha's sandstone, university catalogue entries for Pattadakal materials, and the ASI conservation account for Lotus Mahal's brick-and-lime fabric. References point to the specific monument or component rather than assigning all nearby buildings the same material.

## Media workflow

Fifty candidate photographs were selected for the 25 additions: an overview and a detail/alternate view for each. Each file retains the photographer, licence and licence URL, source page, original URL, dimensions and bytes. Original files are stored locally and responsive WebP copies are generated without enlargement.

Selection excludes plans, stereoscopic pairs, images of neighbouring monuments returned by broad searches, and ambiguous filenames. Very large originals that timed out were replaced with other high-resolution photographs. Wikimedia's HTTP 429 Retry-After is respected; the importer only publishes records after their cover photographs have been prepared and reviewed.

Visual review also rejected the soft Cave 1 medallion photograph `Badami caves Cave One (1).jpg` in favour of the clearer exterior `Badami Cave Temples (1).JPG`. Cave 2's nearly repeated verandah view was replaced by a distinct Trivikrama relief close-up. The rejected local files are outside the selected manifest and are not published.

The misleadingly named `Aihole Lad Khan Temple.jpg` shows a wider hillside landscape rather than a usable temple exterior. The selected replacement is `Lad Khan Temple Aihole.jpg`, whose entrance porch and upper shrine were visually checked against the documented building form.
